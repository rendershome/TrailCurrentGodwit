# Two CAN buses force a build, not a purchase

No off-the-shelf Raspberry Pi 5 or CM5 product meets all of Godwit's head-unit needs at once. The only integrated CM5 touch panel with two onboard CAN ports is the **EDATEC ED-HMI3630 family**: 2 x isolated CAN 2.0B, 9-36 V input, a mic-in/line-out jack and a 10.1" option. It falls short on USB (**only 2 x USB 2.0**), uses an M.2 B-key rather than M-key slot, and is rated to only -20 to 50/60 °C. The CM5 units with dual CAN and more USB, Seeed's reComputer Industrial R21 and EDATEC's ED-IPC3630, are HDMI-only DIN-rail boxes with no DSI connector. The products actually marketed for vehicles with dual CAN (AutoPi TMU CM4, Intrepid neoVI PI) are all CM4-based, which Godwit does not support. **Waveshare sells no dual-CAN panel or carrier.** Every Waveshare CM5 HMI and base with CAN has exactly one isolated MCP2515 channel. Godwit's current approach, a Pi 5 or CM5 with a Waveshare 2-CH CAN FD HAT on SPI, is therefore still the strongest Waveshare-only answer, because it gives both buses on mainline drivers without using a USB port. Whichever route is chosen, three gaps stay open. No candidate documents 12 V load-dump survival, none is rated above 65 °C, and only the 2-CAN CM5 units are rated below 0 °C (to -20/-25 °C). Godwit still needs its own automotive power front end and a powered USB hub for two RTL-SDRs, two dials and USB audio.

## EDATEC's ED-HMI3630 is the lone integrated dual-CAN CM5 panel

Across Chipsee, Waveshare, Seeed, PiLink and EDATEC's other lines, the research found one CM5 all-in-one touch panel family with two CAN ports as standard. The **ED-HMI3630** (sub-models 3632, 3633 and 3634 differ only in their RS232/RS485 mix) has **2 x CAN 2.0B, 3 kV isolated, 10-1000 kbps**, on 6-pin 3.5 mm Phoenix terminals with per-channel status LEDs. It also has a **9-36 V DC input "with reverse polarity protection, overvoltage protection, and overcurrent protection"** ([EDATEC 15.6" datasheet](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/8564/ED-HMI3632-156C-10432-S.pdf); [13.3" datasheet](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/8564/ED-HMI3632-133C-10232-S.pdf)). Classic CAN at 500 kbit/s is well within its range. Unusually for an industrial panel, it has real audio: a **3.5 mm combo jack usable "as MIC IN and LINE OUT"** plus a built-in amplifier and speaker. That could replace the Sound Blaster and free a USB port.

The rest of the specification fits a vehicle less well. The 15.6" and 13.3" datasheets list only **2 x USB 2.0 Type-A**, operating **-20 to 50 °C**, and an **M.2 B-key 2230/2242 SSD** slot that the datasheet does not call NVMe. They state no IP rating and no ignition input, and the watchdog software is "under development" ([15.6" datasheet](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/8564/ED-HMI3632-156C-10432-S.pdf)). The 10.1" (1280x800, -20 to 60 °C) is the size Godwit wants, but its figures come **only from a search-engine summary** of the [Mouser-hosted datasheet](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/1563/EDATEC_01_16_2025_ED_HMI3630_101C_datasheet_en-3541061.pdf), which timed out when the researchers tried to fetch it. Prices are **reseller figures only**: DigiKey Canada lists the 10.1" ED-HMI3632-101C at CAD 994.37 with zero stock ([DigiKey CA](https://www.digikey.ca/en/products/detail/edatec/ED-HMI3632-101C-10832-S/25863650)), and a search snippet put the ED-HMI3634-101C at EUR 398.95 at buyzero ([buyzero](https://buyzero.de/en/products/edatec-ed-hmi3634-101c-industrial-hmi-cm5)), a page that could not be loaded.

The software question matters more for an MIT, 100%-open-source project. The CM5 has no native CAN controller, so the two ports must sit behind an SPI or USB bridge. EDATEC does not name the chip or the device-tree overlay, and no schematics were found. "CAN 2.0B, 10-1000 kbps" with no FD suggests an MCP2515-class part, but that is unconfirmed. A balena forum user reports that the vendor setup adds an EDATEC apt source and a firmware package ([balena forum](https://forums.balena.io/t/integrating-edatac-ed-hmi3630-with-balena/374302), search-summary only). Before the ED-HMI3630 could get AGL board support, someone would have to confirm that its CAN, touch and display paths work with mainline drivers and no vendor blobs.

The neighbouring panels are all single-CAN. Chipsee's PPC-CM5-156 has **1 x CAN FD** and a 15-30 V input ([Chipsee datasheet via SVTronics](https://svtronics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/PPC-CM5-156.pdf)). The PPC-CM5-070 manual maps its one CAN to "CPU SPI0" ([Chipsee manual](https://www.chipsee.com/docs/PCs/Pi/A76/Manuals/Hardware/EPC_PPC-CM5-070.pdf)). Seeed's reTerminal DM is CM4 with one CAN ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2023/06/07/reterminal-dm-a-raspberry-pi-cm4-powered-10-1-inch-hmi-controller/)). Chipsee says products "can be customized during the ordering process", but it lists only OS, CM5 and 4G choices, and no vendor publicly offers a second CAN as a build option. Getting one would take a direct engineering request.

## Dual-CAN CM5 hardware exists, but as headless boxes or CM4 vehicle units

Dual CAN is common on CM5 **box** PCs, which points to an alternative layout: a box behind the dash driving a separate touch display. The closest fit is the **Seeed reComputer Industrial R21**. It has **2 x isolated CAN FD**, **9-36 V DC** input, **-20 to 65 °C** operation, M.2 M-key 2280 NVMe, **3 x USB 3.0 + 1 x USB 2.0 Type-A**, an optional LTC3350 supercap UPS and a 1-255 s hardware watchdog ([Seeed product page](https://www.seeed.cc/product/recomputer-industrial-r); [Seeed wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/recomputer_industrial_R21xx_getting_start/)). The two Seeed sources disagree on audio. The product page lists an audio jack, while the wiki lists a buzzer only, so this needs checking. The R21 has **2 x HDMI and no DSI**, so a touch panel would need HDMI plus USB touch, which uses one of the four USB-A ports. Pricing is quote-only. The **EDATEC ED-IPC3630** has 2 x isolated CAN 2.0B, 9-36 V, -25 to 60 °C and HDMI only, with no audio and **2 x USB 2.0**. Its specs come **only from CNX Software news and the Makerfabs reseller**, because the manufacturer datasheet timed out. On those sources it costs from about $300 at DigiKey or $316 at Makerfabs ([CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2025/03/24/edatec-ed-ipc3630-raspberry-pi-cm5-powered-industrial-pc-di-docan-bus/); [Makerfabs](https://www.makerfabs.com/raspberry-pi-cm5-based-industrial-computer.html)). CNX also reports the PiLink PL-R5 "DIO CAN" (2 x CAN, 9-40 V, -20 to +60 °C, no display; [CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/02/11/pilink-pl-r5-r5m-series-ip20-ip65-industrial-pcs-powered-by-raspberry-pi-cm5/), news only).

Products actually built for vehicles stop at CM4. The **AutoPi TMU CM4** has 2 x CAN FD over SocketCAN, ignition-off controlled shutdown and battery-voltage cut-off, and is "CE/FCC/E-Mark certified". It starts at EUR 420, and its own page gives the input range as both "12-35 V" and "9-35 V" ([AutoPi](https://www.autopi.io/hardware/autopi-tmu-cm4/)). **Intrepid's neoVI PI** puts 4 x CAN FD on a CM4 behind a "5-60V" automotive supply that is "tested and packaged for in-vehicle use" ([Intrepid](https://intrepidcs.com/products/vehicle-network-adapters/neovipi/)). Both show the features a vehicle unit adds, ignition awareness and a wide, crank-tolerant input, but adopting either would mean adding CM4 support to Godwit, and AutoPi has no CM5 product.

None of these units documents ISO 7637-2 or ISO 16750-2 compliance. The researchers' general-knowledge inferences, which no source above cites, are that an unsuppressed 12 V load dump can reach about 80-100 V and that a cold crank can sag well below a 9 V input minimum. The conclusion is that **any 9-36 V or 7-36 V industrial unit needs a TVS-plus-surge-stopper front end and ignition-sense logic ahead of it** in an RV or car. Chipsee's CS-CM5-BOX is the only CM5 unit found with an ignition input, but it has a single CAN and its details come **from a search snippet only** ([Chipsee](https://chipsee.com/product/cs-cm5-box/)).

## Waveshare has many one-CAN boards and one clean dual-CAN HAT

Waveshare's 34-row HMI selection table has **no dual-CAN HMI and no CM5-HMI "B" variant** ([Waveshare selection table](https://www.waveshare.com/pi5-hmi-7a.htm)). The Pi 5 HMIs (PI5-HMI-7A, -080D, -101D) have **no CAN, no audio and a 5 V-only input** ([PI5-HMI-080D](https://www.waveshare.com/pi5-hmi-080d.htm)). The CM5-HMI-7A/8A/10.1A-ACCE are the only Waveshare panels with CAN. They have one isolated channel ("1x CAN supporting 1Mbps"), **7-36 V input**, a supercap hold-up that only works from that input, M.2 M-key NVMe, 2 x USB 2.0 + 2 x USB 3.2, and **a buzzer instead of audio** ([docs.waveshare.com CM5-HMI-7A-ACCE](https://docs.waveshare.com/CM5-HMI-7A-ACCE)). They cost $125.99 to $129.99 without the CM5, and the kit includes a 12 V 4 A supply ([Waveshare](https://www.waveshare.com/cm5-hmi-xa-acce.htm)). Waveshare's CAN tutorial shows the internals: **an MCP2515 on SPI1-0, interrupt GPIO17**, brought up at 500 kbit/s ([CAN tutorial](https://docs.waveshare.com/CM5-HMI-7A-ACCE-Tutorials-CAN)). The board also uses GPIO11 (SPI0 SCLK) for the supercap low-voltage warning and GPIO7 (SPI0 CE1) for the buzzer. With SPI1 taken by the CAN chip and SPI0 compromised, and with no published schematic, **a second CAN on the CM5-HMI realistically has to come over USB**. Waveshare's marketing claim that the 40-pin GPIO is left "free" is not backed by a pinout in the docs.

The CM5 carriers follow the same pattern. The CM5-IO-WIRELESS-BASE, IPCBOX-CM5 and CM5-HMI-BOX-A each have one isolated CAN and dual MIPI ([CM5-IO-WIRELESS-BASE](https://docs.waveshare.com/CM5-IO-WIRELESS-BASE); [IPCBOX-CM5-A](https://docs.waveshare.com/IPCBOX-CM5-A); [CM5-HMI-BOX-A](https://docs.waveshare.com/CM5-HMI-BOX-A)). The most useful base for Godwit has no CAN at all. The **CM5-DUAL-ETH-4G/5G-BASE** has a 7-36 V DC jack, a **40-pin GPIO header**, 2 x 4-lane MIPI for DSI and M.2 M-key NVMe, but **only 2 x USB 3.2 Type-A**. It costs $55.99 to $69.99 ([Waveshare](https://www.waveshare.com/cm5-dual-eth-4g-5g-base.htm)). Its 40-pin header makes it a CM5 host for the same CAN HAT Godwit already runs on the Pi 5.

The dual-CAN pieces are HATs. The **2-CH CAN FD HAT** ($46.99) carries two MCP2518FD controllers behind digital isolation rated "up to 5KV" ([Waveshare](https://www.waveshare.com/2-ch-can-fd-hat.htm)). In **mode A** (Godwit's current setting) CAN_0 is on spi0-0 and CAN_1 on spi1-0. In **mode B**, which takes moving four 0R resistors, both are on SPI0 (CE0/CE1, INT 25/24), which leaves GPIO18-21 free for I2S ([Waveshare wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/2-CH_CAN_FD_HAT)). The wiki still lists only Pi Zero through 4B, and its FAQ reports CAN FD bugs on a Pi OS 6.6.51 kernel. Godwit runs classic CAN on a Pi 5, which already shows the hardware works. The DC input is "8~28V" in one place and "8~26V" in another, and the input is behind a 2 A fuse with no 5 V output rating. **It cannot be counted on as the Pi 5's 5 A supply.** The May 2026 **RS232-RS485-CAN-Board** (about $54.99) also puts both CAN channels on SPI0: one isolated MCP2518FD CAN FD and one isolated MCP2515 classic. It takes 7-36 V and "can work with Pi5 Connector Adapter (B) to power the Raspberry Pi", again with no current rating ([Waveshare wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/RS232-RS485-CAN-Board); price from [CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/05/19/60-kit-transforms-the-raspberry-pi-4-5-into-a-din-rail-industrial-controller-with-isolated-rs232-rs485-and-can-bus/), news). The **2-CH CAN HAT+** defaults to SPI1 and would clash with I2S ([wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/2-CH_CAN_HAT+)). Third parties warn that the MCP2515's two receive buffers can drop frames under heavy load ([Victron](https://github.com/victronenergy/venus/wiki/RaspberryPi-CAN-Interfaces)), so the MCP2518FD parts are the better choice even at 500 kbit/s.

Two Waveshare products fail the open-source requirement or the head-unit requirement. The dual-isolated **USB-CAN-FD** is Windows-only. The -B variant ships vendor C/Python demos with no stated SocketCAN driver, and only a community out-of-tree kernel module exists for the original model ([Waveshare wiki](https://waveshare.com/wiki/USB-CAN-FD); [GitHub seiga-k](https://github.com/seiga-k/waveshare-usb-can-fd-linux)). That would leave an unmaintained module in the AGL/Yocto build. On audio, the WM8960 and DA7212 I2S HATs have only **onboard MEMS mics**: the WM8960 "doesn't feature external MIC input pins even on the 3.5mm jack" ([WM8960 wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/WM8960_Audio_HAT); [DA7212 docs](https://docs.waveshare.com/DA7212-Audio-HAT-B)). A cabled cab mic therefore still needs the USB adapter. No Waveshare product has a 12 V input rated to supply a Pi 5 at 5 A, and none documents transient protection or an ignition input ([PoE HAT (G)](https://www.waveshare.com/poe-hat-g.htm); [UPS HAT (E)](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/UPS_HAT_(E))). Waveshare's DSI panels top out at about 450-500 cd/m² (the 10.1-DSI-TOUCH-B is 500 cd/m² and not optically bonded; [Waveshare](https://www.waveshare.com/10.1-dsi-touch-b.htm)). The only published operating range is **0-60 °C**, on the 10.1" DSI LCD (C) and PI5-HMI-080C wikis ([wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/10.1inch_DSI_LCD_%28C%29)).

## Three Waveshare-only combinations, ranked for Godwit

**Option A is the best all-Waveshare route: CM5 + CM5-DUAL-ETH-4G/5G-BASE + 10.1-DSI-TOUCH-A + 2-CH CAN FD HAT.** It keeps Godwit's proven CAN stack: two isolated MCP2518FD channels on the mainline `mcp251xfd` driver, no USB port used, and the same overlays as the Pi 5 reference. It replaces the Pi 5's 5 V supply problem with the base's own **7-36 V jack** and gets onboard M-key NVMe. The optically bonded 10.1-DSI-TOUCH-A is a better choice than the -B for glare ([Waveshare comparison table](https://www.waveshare.com/10.1-dsi-touch-b.htm)). Mode A is fine while audio stays on USB. Switch to mode B only if an I2S codec is added later. The cost is USB. The base has just **two USB-A ports** for two RTL-SDRs, two dials and the Sound Blaster, so a powered hub is unavoidable. HAT stack height and the BOX-A case's clearance have not been tested.

**Option B stays on the Pi 5 and is the least board-support work: Pi 5 + existing 10.1-DSI-TOUCH + RS232-RS485-CAN-Board (both CANs on SPI0) + Pi5 Connector Adapter (B), or keep the 2-CH CAN FD HAT plus a separate 12 V to 5 V/5 A converter.** The RS232-RS485-CAN-Board's 7-36 V terminal could replace the separate converter, but Waveshare does not rate how much current it delivers to the Pi. One of its channels is the weaker MCP2515, and its DIN-rail form factor will not fit inside an HMI enclosure. In practice, the current reference build plus a third-party automotive 12 V to 5 V/5 A supply remains the safer Pi 5 answer. The Pi 5 has four USB-A ports (two 2.0, two 3.0; [Waveshare HMI page](https://www.waveshare.com/pi5-hmi-7a.htm)), the most of any Waveshare option here, but that is still one short of five devices.

**Option C is the most integrated and the riskiest: CM5-HMI-10.1A-ACCE with its onboard CAN plus a USB second bus.** It brings a 7-36 V input, the supercap power-cut warning on GPIO16 (usable for a systemd clean shutdown, though it is not an ignition sense), an RTC, NVMe and a metal case in one SKU. Its portrait 800x1280 panel needs rotation. The only Waveshare-branded second bus, the USB-CAN-FD, breaks the mainline-driver rule. Replacing it with a non-Waveshare adapter that has a mainline driver fixes that: single-channel candleLight/gs_usb devices have fully open firmware, and dual-channel PEAK PCAN-USB Pro FD or Kvaser 2xHS units run on in-kernel drivers ([gs_usb.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/master/drivers/net/can/usb/gs_usb.c); [USB CAN Kconfig](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/torvalds/linux/master/drivers/net/can/usb/Kconfig)). Either way the adapter takes one of four USB ports, which makes the USB shortfall worse. The panel then has no Waveshare dual-CAN solution and no audio.

For the radio side, every option ends in a hub. Two RTL-SDRs, two ESP32-S3 dials and USB audio make five USB devices. Option A has two ports, Options B and C have four, and Option C also needs one for the CAN adapter. Spending a USB port on CAN, as Option C does, takes it from the receivers. The notes contain **no RF-noise measurements** for any of these boards or hubs, and the USB power budget for two dongles has not been checked. Nooelec's datasheet gives the NESDR SMArt v5 a draw of 270-330 mA ([Nooelec](http://www.nooelec.com/datasheets/100700), cited in the earlier hardware report), so two dongles take roughly 0.54-0.66 A. In practice, use a powered, separately fed hub for the SDRs and put both dongles on short extensions away from the panel and the CM5. Treat the noise floor of the chosen hub and supply as something to measure on the bench. These are general engineering practice, not findings from the research. The SPI-attached CAN HAT is worth keeping partly because it does not compete with the receivers for USB ports.

## Conclusion

The search shows that "industrial Pi panel with dual CAN" is close to an empty category. One EDATEC family qualifies, and it trades away the USB ports and NVMe slot type a radio-centric head unit needs. The Waveshare HAT approach, which looked like a stopgap, is the architecture the market supports. Moving Godwit's reference from a bare Pi 5 to a CM5 on the CM5-DUAL-ETH-4G/5G-BASE would add a 7-36 V input and onboard M-key NVMe without changing the CAN driver stack. The ED-HMI3630 is worth tracking as a possible second board target if EDATEC's CAN and display paths turn out to be mainline-clean.

The bigger finding is that **none of these products is a vehicle product.** None documents load-dump survival or crank ride-through, none is rated above 65 °C, only the 2-CAN CM5 units go below 0 °C, and only CM4 units handle ignition. Whatever compute platform Godwit picks, the work that makes it a head unit is an open-source power and ignition front end: TVS, surge stopper, delayed-off timer, low-battery cut-off and supercap hold-up. That front end is the most valuable hardware Godwit could publish, because no Pi vendor sells it for CM5.
